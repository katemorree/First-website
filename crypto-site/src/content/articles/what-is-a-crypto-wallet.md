A crypto wallet doesn't actually "hold" your coins. Your coins live on the blockchain. The wallet holds the **private key** — the secret that lets you move them.

## Custodial vs self-custody

- **Custodial wallet:** someone else (usually an exchange) holds the keys for you. Easy, but you depend on them staying solvent, secure and willing to let you withdraw.
- **Self-custody wallet:** you hold the keys. Nobody can freeze your funds — but nobody can recover them if you lose your backup.

There's a popular saying: *"Not your keys, not your coins."* It became famous after exchanges like FTX collapsed and customers couldn't withdraw.

## Hot wallets vs cold wallets

| | Hot wallet | Cold wallet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | App on your phone, computer or browser | Physical device that keeps keys offline |
| Cost | Usually free | Typically $60–$250 |
| Convenience | Very high | Lower |
| Security | Good for small amounts | Best for larger amounts |
| Examples | MetaMask, Exodus, Trust Wallet | Ledger, Trezor, Coldcard |

## Your recovery phrase

When you create a self-custody wallet, you'll get a **recovery phrase** (also called a seed phrase) of 12 or 24 words. Anyone with these words can take your crypto.

- Write it on paper or metal — never in a screenshot, notes app or cloud drive
- Never type it into a website
- Never share it with anyone, including "support"

## Which wallet should you use?

- **Just starting with small amounts:** a reputable exchange or a simple mobile wallet is fine.
- **Holding more than you'd be comfortable losing:** a hardware wallet. See our [best hardware wallet guide](/learn/best-hardware-wallet).
- **Using DeFi or NFTs:** a software wallet like MetaMask or Phantom, ideally connected to a hardware wallet.
