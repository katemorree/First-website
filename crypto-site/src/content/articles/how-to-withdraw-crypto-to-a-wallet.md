Withdrawing to your own wallet puts you in control of your keys. It's simple, but one mistake can be permanent — so go slowly the first time.

## Before you start

- Set up your wallet and **back up the recovery phrase** first.
- Make sure the wallet supports the coin **and the network** you'll use.

## Step by step

### 1. Copy your wallet's receive address

Open your wallet, choose the coin, and tap **Receive**. Copy the address. On a hardware wallet, **confirm the address on the device screen**.

### 2. Start the withdrawal on the exchange

Go to **Wallet → Withdraw**, choose the coin and paste the address.

### 3. Choose the correct network

This is where most mistakes happen. USDT, for example, exists on Ethereum, Tron, Solana and other networks. **The network you pick on the exchange must match what your wallet expects.**

| You're sending | Common networks |
| --- | --- |
| BTC | Bitcoin (native) |
| ETH | Ethereum (ERC-20), Arbitrum, Base |
| USDT / USDC | Ethereum, Tron, Solana, others |
| SOL | Solana |

### 4. Check the address

Compare the **first and last 6 characters** with your wallet. Malware can swap copied addresses, and "address poisoning" scams rely on look-alikes.

### 5. Send a small test first

Send a small amount, confirm it arrives, then send the rest. The extra fee is cheap insurance.

### 6. Confirm it arrived

Check your wallet or look up the transaction ID on a block explorer.

## Fees

Exchanges charge a withdrawal fee, which varies by coin and network. Cheaper networks are fine — as long as your wallet supports them.
