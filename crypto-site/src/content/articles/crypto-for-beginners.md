Cryptocurrency can feel confusing because people explain it with jargon. This guide explains the basics in plain English, shows you the real risks, and walks you through a sensible first purchase.

> **Before you start:** crypto is high risk. Prices can fall 50% or more in a short time, and you can lose everything you put in. Only use money you can afford to lose. Nothing here is financial advice.

## What is cryptocurrency?

A cryptocurrency is digital money that runs on a **blockchain** — a shared record of transactions kept by thousands of computers around the world instead of one bank.

Because no single company controls the record, you can send crypto to anyone, anywhere, at any time, without asking permission. That's the core idea.

**Bitcoin** (2009) was the first cryptocurrency and is still the largest. **Ethereum** came next and added "smart contracts" — programs that run on the blockchain and power apps for lending, trading and more. Thousands of other coins exist, but most are far riskier than these two.

## How does it actually work?

You don't need to understand the technical details to use crypto safely, but three ideas help:

- **Wallets hold keys, not coins.** Your coins live on the blockchain. A wallet stores the secret key that proves they're yours.
- **Transactions are final.** If you send crypto to the wrong address or to a scammer, there's usually no way to get it back.
- **Networks charge fees.** Every transaction pays a small fee to the network. Fees vary with how busy it is.

## The main risks (read this part)

- **Volatility.** Bitcoin has fallen more than 70% from its highs several times. Smaller coins can fall 90%+ and never recover.
- **Scams.** Fake giveaways, "investment managers" on social media, impersonated support staff and fake apps are extremely common.
- **Platform risk.** Exchanges can be hacked, freeze withdrawals or go bankrupt — as FTX did in 2022.
- **Your own mistakes.** Lost recovery phrases and wrong addresses cause a lot of permanent losses.
- **Leverage.** Borrowing to trade multiplies losses. Most beginners should avoid it entirely.

## A sensible way to start

### 1. Learn the basics first

Read how [crypto wallets work](/learn/what-is-a-crypto-wallet) and [how to keep crypto safe](/learn/how-to-keep-crypto-safe). Twenty minutes of reading prevents most beginner mistakes.

### 2. Pick a reputable exchange

An exchange is where you swap normal money for crypto. For beginners, ease of use, security and regulation matter more than the lowest fees. See our [best exchanges for beginners](/learn/best-crypto-exchanges-for-beginners) or the full [exchange comparison](/exchanges).

### 3. Secure your account

Use a unique password, turn on two-factor authentication with an authenticator app (not SMS), and set an anti-phishing code if the exchange offers one.

### 4. Start small

Buy a small amount of Bitcoin or Ethereum. Many people spread purchases out over time (called dollar-cost averaging, or DCA) instead of buying all at once. Our [DCA calculator](/tools/dca-calculator) shows how your average cost works out.

### 5. Decide where to store it

Small amounts can stay on a reputable exchange while you learn. For larger amounts, consider moving them to a wallet you control — see [how to withdraw crypto to a wallet](/learn/how-to-withdraw-crypto-to-a-wallet).

## Common beginner mistakes

- Buying a coin because it's trending on TikTok or X
- Sharing a recovery phrase with "support" (real support will never ask)
- Using leverage before understanding liquidation
- Investing money needed for rent or bills
- Ignoring taxes until the end of the year

## Key takeaways

Crypto is a new, volatile asset class. Start with education, use reputable platforms, secure your accounts, keep amounts small, and never put in more than you can afford to lose.
