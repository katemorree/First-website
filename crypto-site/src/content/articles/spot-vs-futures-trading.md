When you buy on the **spot** market, you own the actual coin. When you trade **futures**, you're trading a contract that tracks the coin's price — usually with borrowed money (leverage).

## Side-by-side

| | Spot | Futures (perpetual) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Do you own the coin? | Yes | No — you hold a contract |
| Can you withdraw it to a wallet? | Yes | No |
| Leverage | No | Yes, often up to 100x+ |
| Can you profit when price falls? | No (only by selling) | Yes, by going "short" |
| Liquidation risk | No | Yes |
| Ongoing costs | None | Funding payments every few hours |
| Beginner friendly | Yes | No |

## How spot trading works

You pay $100 and receive $100 worth of Bitcoin (minus fees). If Bitcoin falls 20%, your holding is worth $80. You can wait for it to recover or sell — but you can't be forced out.

## How futures trading works

Most crypto futures are **perpetual contracts** — they have no expiry date. You post **margin** (collateral) and can open a position larger than your margin using leverage.

To keep the contract price close to the real price, traders pay each other a **funding rate** every few hours. When most traders are long, longs usually pay shorts.

If the price moves against you far enough, the exchange **liquidates** your position and you lose the margin. Read [how crypto leverage works](/learn/how-crypto-leverage-works) for the maths.

## Which should you choose?

- **Learning or investing long term:** spot.
- **Hedging an existing holding, or experienced active trading:** futures can be a tool — with strict risk limits and low leverage.

> Leveraged trading is high risk. Many retail traders lose money. Never trade futures with money you can't afford to lose.
